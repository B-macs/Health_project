The Flexibility screen shows one number.
It is two numbers multiplied — and one of them scores you down for being too flexible.

Every other sector in this app treats higher as better. This one cannot, and the reason is in the clinical file rather than in the design: Beighton 6/9 means stability here is muscular, not ligamentous, so a joint reached with nothing to stop it is the hazard the profile names — not an achievement to reward.

The claim

The athlete's brief was one number out of 100 where 100 is a perfect flexible body, assembled from two inputs that share no unit: degrees from a gym goniometer, and a 1–100 self-rating of how far into a yoga pose he can get. His own framing, and the reason the design is what it is:

Athlete, 2026-08-05

“The overall perfect flexible body is having the flexibility and the control to do the stretch correctly. It is always two things.”

So the score is two things, multiplied:

region  = sqrt(RANGE × CONTROL)
overall = Σ(weightr × confidencer × scorer) / Σ(weightr × confidencer)

The geometric mean is the argument, not an implementation detail. An arithmetic mean lets a 100 on one axis carry a 20 on the other to a respectable 60. This gives 45, and a zero on either axis annihilates the region — which is the correct behaviour when the two things are jointly necessary. Both axes stay visible all the way to the screen, because “Range 100, Control 66 → 81” says something the number 81 alone does not.

The transform that inverts the scale

The athlete's depth scale runs 1 = can barely enter the position to 100 = at the physical limit, no stretch sensation left. That 100 is not the overall score's 100. control_score() is the transform between them, and it is the whole reason two axes exist rather than one blended number.

Score first, then average — never the reverse

The hamstring region sees three poses. Averaging the ratings and scoring once gives a completely different answer from scoring each pose and averaging the scores, and only one of them is honest:

Average, then score — wrong
100
ratings 25, 76, 64 → weighted mean 55.2 → inside the ideal band → scores 100. The 25 — a straddle fold he can barely enter, and the single most informative reading in the whole assessment — vanishes completely.
Score, then average — right
65.5
each pose scored first → 25.0, 82.0, 100.0 → weighted mean 65.5. The straddle survives into the region total, which is the only reason the region reads as a problem at all.
The same trap, one layer down — found while hand-checking

The region's direction label had the identical bug in the first build. It was derived by averaging the ratings, so hamstrings — mean rating 55.2, inside the band — came out labelled Ideal while scoring 65. A region cannot be ideal and score 65.

Direction is now attributed from where the lost points came from: sum the weighted deficit on each side and take the larger. Hamstrings gives 37.5 restricted against 10.8 unstable → Restricted. Back gives 31.9 against 123.0 → No stop, which is the correct read of a region whose deficit is almost entirely end-range passive twists. test_direction_is_attributed_from_lost_points_not_from_the_mean_rating pins it.

Hip 33° means one thing as internal rotation (normal 35–45, so: low, and it corroborates the tight posterior capsule already in imbalances.overactive_tight) and something else entirely as a Thomas test (normal 10–20, where 33° would be excellent — the opposite finding, and flatly contradicting the “Low” the gym printed).

Every entry therefore carries protocol=None and provisional=True; bands are assumed from the most likely protocol, marked * on screen, and their confidence multiplied by 0.6. Setting protocol clears all of that with no other edit.

Why Lat Flex has no band at all

The gym calls 20–21° Normal. Trunk lateral flexion is normally 35–45°, where 20° would be markedly low. So either the label does not mean trunk side-bend, or those are not population norms. A band guessed out of a contradiction is worse than no band — the Range axis is absent and the region scores on Control alone.

The perfect-symmetry question

Three regions differ left-to-right by 1–3°. Two are exactly equal. Chest 106/106 is the least likely reading on the sheet: three right anterior dislocations, a failed capsular repair and a Latarjet coracoid transfer (patient_profile.py finding #6), and a post-Latarjet shoulder characteristically loses external rotation and horizontal abduction on the operated side. Either one measurement was mirrored into both columns, or the protocol does not isolate the shoulder. Flagged symmetry_suspect, not resolved — one visit settles it.

What the score says today

The hamstring reconciliation

The gym reads 89°/86° and calls it Normal, which agrees with a straddle fold rated 25/100 rather than contradicting it. Long-sitting upright is already ~90° of hip flexion with the knee straight, so at 86–89° he is at the hamstring limit merely sitting up with his legs out, and every further degree of fold has to come from the spine.

So the finding is normal length with no reserve, not shortness — an ordinary hamstring under an exceptional lumbar spine. The earlier wording in patient_profile.py said “restriction” and implied short; it has been restated. And the reconciliation holds only if that reading is a straight-leg raise, which is exactly what the unrecorded protocol prevents anyone from knowing.

The screen is not broken, because every direction ships a text pill (“Ideal”, “Restricted”, “End range, no stop”) and is never colour-alone. But the colour is carrying none of the load it appears to.

Not changed here. These tokens are shared with the Strength and Metabolism screens, so re-stepping them is a visual change to two shipped sectors and needs a decision, not a drive-by.

What this refuses to do

  • No flexibility age in years. The gym ships one — 28 against “Real age: 31” — but it was measured 2025-01-17 when he was 30. It compares a stale measurement against a live chronological age, so the gap was −2 at measurement, displays as −3, and widens every birthday without anybody moving a millimetre. Same defect class as the typed InBody height, same refusal as services/body_composition.py's.
  • No scoring the gym's Low/Normal verdicts. Kept verbatim as provenance and shown on the screen; nothing computes from them. Their norm tables are undisclosed, so scoring them would import the vendor's reference into ours and then double-count it against our own band.
  • No filling an empty region from the other instrument, or from a training note. Squat Depth is empty on both axes and is reported as empty. The 2025 log's “mobility excellent, hits depth easily” is a qualitative note, not a measurement.
  • No region scored without a reference band.
  • Nothing here reaches the engine. Not the traffic light, not ACWR, not readiness, not the volume recommendation. services/rules.py remains the only thing that constrains movement.

What has to be built

  1. The protocol list from the gym. One question. Lifts Range confidence from 60% of its stale value to full, and settles whether the hamstring reconciliation above is real. Nothing in the code changes — set protocol, confirm the band, provisional clears itself.
  2. A re-scan, asking explicitly for Squat Depth, Back and Shoulders. All three are empty; Back and Shoulders carry 35% of the overall between them and currently run on Control alone. The existing scan is 565 days old and halves in weight every 365, so it counts for 34% of a fresh reading.
  3. Re-rate the 22 poses. Free, repeatable, and the only axis that is current. Three poses carry specific retests attached to them in services/yoga.py.
  4. Decide the status-trio question from Finding 1 — app-wide, affects three screens.
  5. Then, and only then, a trend. One instrumented date cannot have one. The progress chart the gym draws with a single point in January is the thing this screen deliberately does not imitate.
