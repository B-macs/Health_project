Architecture · Flexibility v2

Stop scoring joints. Score the skill, and name the rung that’s holding it back.

v1 averaged eight body regions into one number and buried the athlete’s worst capacity in it. v2 takes the minimum, because only the lowest rung on a ladder limits anything — and it reports which rung that is, which is the only output that tells you what to train.

Why v1 failed, in one line

The athlete’s own objection killed it: “we know my hips are stuck in flexion with my back arched and this is a huge issue for me, and yet my flexibility score is nearly 80 for hips.”

He was right, and the arithmetic shows exactly how. The hip score averaged fourteen pose-contributions spanning five unrelated capacities — and Deep Lunge, the only pose testing hip extension, scored 100. Averaging is what let a maxed-out rung hide a broken one.

The structural fix

A ladder has no average. If your ankle stops you at 40, it does not matter that your hamstrings are at 95 — you still cannot squat. The score for a skill is min(rungs), and the name of that rung is published beside it.

The four layers

Layer 1 · Goal
Skills, not joints
“Hip mobility” is as meaningless as “hip strength” — the joint does too many things. A skill is a position you can either achieve or not. Four are in scope, chosen for transfer to the lifts already in the block, not for gymnastics.
Layer 2 · Diagnosis
Ladder rungs
Each skill sits on a ladder of candidate limiters — muscle length, joint capsule, nerve tension, strength deficit, technique. The 13 regions agreed earlier are these rungs. They are diagnostics, never a score.
Layer 3 · Measurement
Three readings per rung
Passive, isometric, active — the same position measured three ways. The passive−active gap is the number that matters here, and it replaces v1’s self-rated “control” question with two objective readings.
Layer 4 · Scheduling
A daily flexibility window — good / ok / poor — computed from the existing training log. Advisory only; nothing here reaches the engine.

The ladder, drawn

Three measures, and why the gap is the point

The blueprint this is built on is written for people who lack range. This athlete is Beighton 6/9 — the assisted half of that method largely solves a problem he does not have. What survives, and what matters here, is the passive−active gap.

What this replaces

v1’s CONTROL axis asked the athlete “did you own that position?” and scored the answer. That was a self-rating standing in for a measurement. Passive and active are two readings of the same position taken minutes apart; the gap falls out of them. Strictly better, and it costs one extra reading per rung.

A gap in the agreed 13

Nothing isolates the lats, and they are the classic overhead limiter. chest_horizontal catches pec length and shoulders_overhead is the composite — neither separates the lat out. The source method does: the lats are the only ones of the three crossing the lower back, so rounding the lumbar spine while reaching overhead isolates them relatively.

Flagging rather than silently adding a 14th rung. It is a real hole in shoulder_flexion’s ladder and it is the skill the athlete named as a problem.

The instrument: 13 rungs, each with a lock

Every test names a LOCK — the thing that must not move, because an unlocked joint lets a neighbour substitute and the test measures nothing. This is the failure that broke the model twice, so it is now an explicit field. Measurements are taped distances, not eyeballed angles, because distance is what the same person alone reproduces in three months.

Four tests replace a contraindicated standard

Not warned about — replaced, with something measuring the same capacity safely. The seated forward fold, sit-and-reach and standing toe-touch all go (end-range lumbar flexion, covered annulus tears at L3/4 and L4/5) and become a supine straight-leg raise. The prone Ely quad test goes (a tight rectus femoris tilts the pelvis and drives lumbar extension) and becomes side-lying with the pelvis hugged shut. The doorway pec stretch and supine 90/90 both go — they hang the anterior capsule on an external frame, which is the post-Latarjet apprehension position — and become the wall slide he already uses.

Protocol rules

  • Measure cold. No warm-up. A warm measurement reads the viscoelastic effect, which is gone in hours; a cold one isolates the durable change. This is the difference between tracking progress and tracking whether you happened to stretch that morning.
  • Same surface, same marks, same time of day. Trace the foot outline once and photograph it. Freeze the phone position.
  • Bilateral rungs record L and R separately and never average them — the asymmetries are the clinical content.
  • ~35–45 min, every 6–12 weeks. Not a weekly ritual.

Scheduling: a window, not a rule

The source claims flexibility training generates central fatigue through the same downstream pathway as strength training, delayed by hours to days. The mechanism is stated well past what the evidence carries and is recorded as motivation, not fact — the same treatment services/sleep_fusion.py gives the abandoned quiet-wake rule. The scheduling heuristic is worth encoding regardless, and it is computable from the training log the app already holds.

A contradiction with what the app does today

views/training.py:1472 offers a yoga session on rest days via suggest_for_day("rest_day") — the slot this rule calls worst.

They are not the same thing: a restorative flow on a rest day is fine, a training session aimed at adaptation is what should move. But nothing in the code distinguishes them right now, so the sessions need an intent flag before the window can be honest.

None of the 22 yoga depth-ratings survive. They answer a question that is neither passive range nor active range (“how far did I get and how much did I feel”), and reinterpreting them would be inventing data. They are kept in full, with the athlete’s verbatim notes, as a dated historical instrument — provenance, not input.

So the assessment is not an enhancement to the model. Until it is run once, it is the model.

Where this sits among the goals

Athlete, 2026-08-05

“It needs to be an underlying goal, not as strong as running 10 km in October, but it should be part of getting the whole body in a better position, as this will limit future injuries and provide better overall strength and wellness rather than just getting strong.”

That makes flexibility a standing objective, not a block. The profile already has exactly one thing shaped like this — the hypermobility entry, whose training implication reads “a standing modifier on every block, not something that resolves or gets reassessed away like a healing injury.” Flexibility v2 takes the same shape, and the same precedence:

  1. Clinical constraints — rules.py, the MRI, the physio. Never overridden.
  2. The 10 km on 2026-10-11 — the dated goal with a deadline.
  3. Flexibility — standing, undated, no deadline. Yields the calendar to the race, but is never finished.

Concretely that means one to two sessions a week of three to five exercises, targeting whichever rung is currently lowest, scheduled into good windows and never displacing race work. And the rationale is a claim the app can eventually test rather than assert: if a lift is limited by position rather than by force, then clearing the limiting rung should raise the lift without any change in strength. services/strength.py already tracks the strength side independently, so the two series can be compared.

What gets built

  1. flexibility_baselines.py — rewritten. Skills, their ladders, the 13 rung definitions with lock/measurement/reference, and the gym goniometry demoted to provenance.
  2. services/flexibility.py — rewritten. min-not-mean, three measures per rung, the passive−active gap, and limiting_rung as a first-class output. The old band_score / CONTROL_BAND downslope is deleted, with a guard test so it cannot return.
  3. The assessment capture path — 13 tests × up to 3 readings × L/R, with the protocol text shown at the point of entry, cold-measurement reminder included.
  4. The upper-body yoga session — 17 poses, holds kept under 30 s so the app is not quietly making the endurance prescription that is reserved for the physio on 2026-08-16.
  5. The screen — skill score, the named limiting rung, the gap per rung, and the window.
  6. Tests and docs — and a docs/resume.md FLEXIBILITY section rewritten against this.
