Core confidence — no 2025 baseline is comparable in kilograms

This week proves the distinction you are drawing, without anyone having to argue for it. Tonnage for the week of 3 Aug is 0 kg — −5,595 kg, −100.0% on the week. The Overall Strength Score for the same week is 50.0 points, 0.0 change. Nothing was lifted; nothing was lost. That is the whole separation, on real data, on the week you are actually in.

It is not a rest week either. There was a training day on 3 Aug — 29 unloaded reps of it. Zero tonnage is a true statement about eligible loaded work, so the unloaded reps are printed beside the zero rather than left to read as “nothing happened”.

Why the overall can be fixed at 50 while the split is unknown — it is not a compromise, it is arithmetic. Overall is Σ sharer × indexr, and the shares sum to 1. With every regional index starting at 50, the overall is 50 whatever the shares turn out to be. Calibration can run for months without the headline number moving a point, and the day it finishes nothing jumps.

The same identity gives you both views for free. Contribution in points is sharer × indexr, which sums to the overall by construction; contribution percent is that over the total. Flip Scale to the 2025 reference and every index is 100, so the three contributions read 32.3 + 18.6 + 49.1 = 100 points — which is the line in your spec. Today they read 16.2 + 9.3 + 24.5 = 50.0.

What was wrong, and what changed. The three regions were showing their index as the headline number — 50 · 50 · 50 — which reads as a total of 150 against an overall of 50. Index never summed to anything; it was the wrong number in the biggest slot. Every region now leads with its contribution in points and its percentage and carries the bare index underneath. The totals row that spelled out 16.2 + 9.3 + 24.5 = 50.0 has since been taken off the screen as redundant — the arithmetic is unchanged and still enforced in code, it is simply no longer narrated to you.

Your instinct of 50 is better than the raw measurement, and here is why. Against real 2025 peaks the lifts you are currently cleared to do read 67.8 — upper body 72.6, lower body 64.6. That holds across every assumption about how close to failure your 2025 PR sets were (69–74 upper across RIR 0–3), so it is not an artefact of the estimator.

But the 2025 log also contains a Back Squat at 80 kg × 5 and a Front Squat at 60 kg × 8, and there is no current equivalent of either — they are exactly the high-compression patterns the L5/S1 findings rule out. Carry those two in as strength you cannot currently express and lower body falls from 64.6 to 41.3, and the overall to roughly 54. The honest number sits between 54 and 68, and 50 is the conservative end of it. Keeping 50 costs nothing and the anchor is replaceable.

Confidence is three things multiplied

quantity × comparability × consistency. Upper body scores 0.67 × 0.95 × 0.72 = 0.46; lower 0.58 × 0.77 × 0.83 = 0.37. Both sit under the 0.70 exit, so both display as provisional.

Consistency is doing real work: upper body's four lifts spread 37–87, and it is Face Pull at 37 dragging it — a machine-dependent movement the 2025 log itself records as “15–21 kg depending on machine”. That is a measurement problem, not a weak rear delt.

Shares blend a prior with evidence

share = (1−k)·prior + k·evidence, renormalised. As confidence rises the evidence takes over; at k = 0 you get the prior untouched, which is exactly what core needs right now.

The prior is stated, not measured — upper 0.35 / core 0.20 / lower 0.45. Core is above a generic split on purpose, because the 2025 analysis names it: “the abs > lower back strength ratio is one of the big reasons the back keeps getting injured.”

One dropdown, five series, one display

The picker sits on the screen, not in this review chrome — it is an app control. Selecting a metric changes the y-axis, the value labels, the unit, the readout and the tooltip together. Points for the score, kilograms for the four tonnage views.

Each tonnage view scales its own axis: core tops out at 240 kg, lower body at 3,000. That the two look alike at a glance is precisely why kilograms are never compared across regions anywhere else on this page.

Tonnage can never move the score, by construction

They share no term. Tonnage is Σ load × reps over completed loaded sets; the score moves on a median-of-three-weeks estimated 1RM trend against fixed 2025 baselines. Adding a fourth set of an easy weight raises tonnage and leaves the e1RM untouched.

Your four cases all fall out of that, rather than needing a rule each: higher tonnage with flat strength (more sets at the same load), lower tonnage with rising strength (fewer, heavier sets), zero tonnage holding strength (this week), and strength climbing under a deliberate volume cap — which is exactly the situation Stage 2A is in.

Coverage, not physiology, drives the raw evidence

If shares came from exercise coverage alone, core would take 4.1% — because it has one exercise against upper body's four and lower body's four. That is the “unequal numbers of exercises” failure in your list, and it is why the evidence term is held back by confidence rather than used directly.

Two concepts, both on the card, only one in the big slot

Contribution answers “how much of the overall does this region account for” — 16.2 / 9.3 / 24.5 pts, i.e. 32.3 / 18.6 / 49.1 %. It is the headline, because it is the number that has to add up. Index answers “how does this region compare with its own 2025 self” — all three start at 50, and it sits underneath, named.

Putting index in the headline is what made the card read as 50 + 50 + 50 = 150. Index never summed to anything: three regions each at half their own 2025 peak is exactly what an overall of 50 means, but only the contribution column is a division of the whole.

They move independently. A region can climb its own index while its share falls, simply because another region climbed faster.

The rounding has to be handled in code, not left to the formatter

0.323 / 0.186 / 0.491 × 50 rounds to 16.2 + 9.3 + 24.6 = 50.1. Round every part but the last, then let the last absorb the remainder — per column, independently, so points total the overall and percents total 100.

The residue, stated rather than hidden: lower body shows 24.5 pts and 49.1%, and 24.5 ÷ 50 is 49.0. No rounding scheme makes both columns exact and every row self-consistent; the sums are the rule you set, so they win.

Tonnage — the second metric, and why it is kept apart

The formula, and what is eligible

exercise tonnage = load × reps × completed sets
                 = Σ over completed sets of (load × reps)     # what the log allows

weekly tonnageᵣ  = Σ eligible loaded sets in sector r, Mon–Sun
overall tonnage  = upper + core + lower                       # by construction

eligible = a completed working set carrying a real external load
excluded = warm-ups · rehabilitation drills · every unloaded movement

The second form is what the per-set log actually supports and is identical to the first whenever the sets are uniform — 3 × 10 × 40 kg = 1,200 kg either way. Using the per-set sum means a top set at a different weight is not silently rounded into the others.

No decay, ever. Tonnage is a statement about one week. A week with no eligible loaded work is 0 kg, and that is true rather than alarming — the week of 3 Aug had a real training day in it, which is why the unloaded reps are reported beside the zero instead of being left to look like nothing happened.

Primary sector, one each, counted once

Every logged exercise carries exactly one sector, from training_constants.EXERCISE_BODY_REGION. A compound lift's whole tonnage goes to its primary sector — an RDL is not split between lower body and core — which is what makes upper + core + lower = overall an identity rather than an approximation.

How the split is calculated

The identity everything rests on

overall        = Σ shareᵣ × indexᵣ           (Σ shareᵣ = 1)
contributionᵣ  = shareᵣ × indexᵣ            → Σ contributionᵣ = overall
percentᵣ       = contributionᵣ / overall

today: every indexᵣ = 50  →  overall = 50, for ANY share split
2025:  every indexᵣ = 100 →  overall = 100, contributions sum to 100 points

Index is not a contribution and must never occupy the contribution's slot. Three regions each at 50 against their own 2025 peaks is precisely what an overall of 50 means — index is a comparison, not a share, and asking it to total 50 is a category error. The card leads with sharer × indexr in points and percent, and carries the index below it as a separate, labelled figure.

A share is not a diagnosis. Lower body taking 49.1% says the other two regions account for less of the total — which follows from coverage, exercise selection and the stated prior as much as from physiology. No imbalance claim is made from these percentages, and none at all while confidence is under 0.70. The muscle-balance section below is a separate, clinically-sourced list and stays that way.

Layer 1 · measured performance, per exercise

RIR      = max(0, 10 − RPE)
eff_reps = reps + RIR
e1RM     = weight × (1 + eff_reps / 30)          # Epley

indexᵢ   = e1RM_now / e1RM_2025 × 100            # never raw kg across regions
indexᵣ   = Σ(wᵢ · indexᵢ) / Σ(wᵢ)                # wᵢ = EXERCISE_MOVEMENT_WEIGHT

Normalisation happens before anything is combined. A hip thrust and a face pull never meet as kilograms — only as percentages of their own 2025 selves.

Layer 2 · confidence, per region

quantity      = min(1, observations / 12)
comparability = Σ(wᵢ · cmpᵢ) / Σ(wᵢ)             # same lift 1.0 … no baseline 0.0
consistency   = 1 − min(1, stdev(indexᵢ) / mean(indexᵢ))
k             = quantity × comparability × consistency

Layer 3 · share, blended

shareᵣ = normalise( (1 − kᵣ) · priorᵣ  +  kᵣ · evidenceᵣ )

prior     = upper 0.35 · core 0.20 · lower 0.45   # stated, from the 2025 analysis
evidence  = region's movement-weight mass / total # coverage, not physiology

Layer 4 · the weekly model, unchanged

Overall still moves by the rule you approved — measured performance can only push it up; the only downward force is detraining decay — except that decay is suspended while calibrating. Each regional index runs the same step independently, then the shares are recomputed so the contributions still sum to the overall.
