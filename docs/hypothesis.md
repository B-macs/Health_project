# The Working Hypothesis

**Version 1 — 2026-08-17.** Requested by the athlete: *"taking the patient
profile details, and the current testing, and coming up with your own
hypothesis. Then block by block we hone in on that hypothesis. It needs to
factor how the hypothesis would break and what would strengthen it."*

This document is the reasoning channel that replaces physiotherapist
confirmation (retired as a gate 2026-08-17, `stage_transitions`). It works the
way the flexibility battery's `EXPECTED_PATTERN` works: predictions are
written BEFORE the data arrives, so a borderline reading cannot be read toward
the answer already in mind. At every block boundary the open predictions are
scored — CONFIRMED / BROKEN / UNTESTED, with the reading — and the hypothesis
is revised, strengthened, or split. A hypothesis that cannot name what would
break it is not one; that standard was applied to the findings and the
movement rules this week, and this page is held to it too.

## The hypothesis: GRIP, NOT SHORTNESS

> **This is a hypermobile body (Beighton 6/9) that learned to manufacture
> stability by static over-gripping — substituting continuous low-grade
> co-contraction for graded, task-specific motor control. The cost surfaces
> under sustained stillness, not under dynamic load. Tissue length, joint
> range and muscular capacity are largely adequate; most of what has looked
> like shortness or weakness is protective tone and mis-sequenced
> recruitment.**

Stated as three sub-claims, because they break separately:

**H1 — The driver is static grip.** With ligamentous restraint reduced,
stability is muscular and continuous. Structures that should fire briefly and
release instead hold — upper glutes, hip flexors, left trapezius — and the
symptom mechanism is what continuous holding does to a muscle: occlusion,
ischemia, the positional cramp. Load is not the enemy; stillness is.

**H2 — Capacity and range are mostly already there.** Underneath the grip,
strength and joint range are at or above what the conservative loading has
assumed. Apparent restrictions are largely guarding — reversible by
activation and graded exposure on a timescale of weeks, where true tissue
change takes months.

**H3 — Real local deficits exist, and they are the exceptions, not the
pattern.** The adductor group reads genuinely short (battery Pattern D), and
the seated pelvic tilt is genuinely unavailable. The hypothesis does not deny
local findings; it claims the *system-level* story is grip, with a short list
of true tissue-level work.

## Why this and not something else — the evidence lines

Each line is dated and checkable; none is an impression.

1. **Every symptom in the record onset under static exposure, and load keeps
   being exonerated.** The 2026-07-06 strain: *holding* a corrected walking
   posture — "isometric contraction in a shortened position for the duration
   of the walk". The interscapular symptom: desk stillness, solved to
   perfusion-limited left trapezius (2026-08-13), where movement, face pulls
   and heat relieve and eight hours of holding does not — "the issue is
   stillness/stiffness, not weakness". The pec cramp: a *held* prayer
   position, never once during pressing. Meanwhile: AU predicts no symptom, a
   10 km hike produced nothing, six interscapular reports fell on weekdays
   with clean weekends. The symptom topology tracks postures held, not loads
   moved.

2. **The original assessment already named the mechanism** — "upper glutes
   and hip flexors over-grip to create artificial stability → compressed
   joints and snapping tendons" (2026-06-28). What it did not say is that
   grip, not the compressed structures, is the system-level lesion. Seven
   weeks of treating the *structures* (capsule stretch at the wrong tissue,
   daily releases past their met criterion) produced less than one day of
   measuring them.

3. **The first day of real measurement found capacity high and range normal.**
   Hip internal rotation >45° both sides (the "tight posterior capsule" of
   finding #2, refuted in sixty seconds). Single-arm Y-hold 105 s against a
   40 s reference. Five of six findings quiet or resolved on first testing —
   including the Coxa Saltans click, which resolved through *motor-path
   retraining* (finding #4's neutral-rotation practice), exactly the fix H1
   predicts and a length- or strength-based model does not.

4. **The 2025 log is a recruitment story, not a capacity story.** "Strong
   enough in isolated work … under-responsive during compound lifts";
   "very dependent on warm-up to fire properly"; the brace "collapses on
   rep 6+" while the legs still have reps in them. A weak muscle is weak in
   isolation too. A gripped or mis-sequenced one is strong alone and absent
   in the orchestra.

5. **The counter-datum is real and stays in view** (H3): bent-knee leverage
   20/21.5 cm against a 10 cm target is the battery saying the adductor group
   itself is short — the one reading that does NOT fit pure grip, which is
   why it gets its own sub-claim and its own falsifier below.

## How it breaks, and what strengthens it

Five trials are live right now. Each carries a prediction registered today —
before its data exists — and the instrument that scores it.

| # | Trial (instrument) | Prediction if the hypothesis is TRUE | What BREAKS it |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Release withdrawal** — rest days release-free; weekly grip grade, days 10/17/24 | Grip stays ≤ grade 1 for the whole block without daily release: the tone was compensable, not load-bearing | Right grip climbs to 2+ or hip-crest symptoms return → the release was doing structural work; H1 weakens, the pair returns daily |
| P2 | **Graded flexion exposure** (start pending) — finding #3's fold count, weekly | Four weeks of progressive flexion stays at zero releases, zero pain: the disc is not the governor | Lumbar-base releases or pain return under load → the Nov-2025 MRI is still determining; the list decision gets revisited, and that is written here in advance |
| P3 | **Running introduction** — six runs, stage_2b running_tolerance | All six clean; no Sartorius signal. Historic strains were overuse dose errors, not tissue fragility | Any left anterior-hip signal → a true local vulnerability (H3 grows, H2 shrinks); the build stops per its own rule |
| P4 | **Heavy top sets** — day 22, first Epley-valid e1RMs | Top sets come in AT or ABOVE prescription at RPE ≤ 8: capacity was underrated by symptom-era caution | Prescribed weights grind or fail → capacity genuinely low; H2 takes the hit |
| P5 | **Bunkie baseline** — day 28, five lines timed | High absolute times; right below left on the lateral line by a modest gap (mirrors the Y-hold) | Short holds across the board, or asymmetry opposite to the symptom side → capacity or attribution is wrong |
| P6 | **The battery over blocks** — pattern D's rungs | The active–passive gap and strength-at-depth move on a timescale of WEEKS under isometric/activation work, faster than passive range grows — guarding releasing, not tissue lengthening | Only passive range moves, slowly, with the gap constant → the restriction is length after all; H3's list grows to include it |
| P7 | **Symptom topology** — monthly, from check-ins + session notes | Symptoms keep tracking static exposure (desk days, held positions) and stay decoupled from AU/strain | A symptom that tracks load — worse after heavy days, quiet on desk days → H1's core is wrong |

**Standing strengtheners** (each occurrence adds weight without a formal
trial): a symptom relieved by movement or contraction of the affected tissue;
a "tight" reading that normalises within one session of activation work; a
strength jump too fast to be hypertrophy (recruitment unlocking).

## The questions ledger

The athlete's framing, kept as the working style: when a signal is ambiguous,
write the *discriminating question* rather than the conclusion.

- **"If pull-ups get stronger but my chest press stays the same"** — before
  reading it as pec weakness: is the scapula positioned or held (finding #6's
  eccentric control)? Is the right pec-minor/scar region (2026-08-07 cramp,
  released since) restricting the press position? Is the incline setup
  masking a left-tilt compensation? Instruments: single-arm Y-hold trend,
  press notes on tilt, the prayer-position weekly re-check.
- **Squat stalls while RDL climbs** → brace endurance, not leg strength —
  check the Bunkie anterior line trend before adding leg volume (the 2025
  breakdown was "brace collapses rep 6+", never the legs).
- **A flexibility rung stalls** → is it length or is it fear-of-position?
  Compare active vs passive at the same rung: passive far ahead of active =
  guarding (H2); both capped together = length (H3).
- **A symptom appears** → first question is always the exposure ledger, not
  the training log: what was HELD in the last 48 h? (Every symptom so far has
  answered to that question first.)

## Block-by-block protocol

At each block boundary (next: **day 28, 2026-09-13**):

1. Score every open prediction — CONFIRMED / BROKEN / UNTESTED, with the
   reading and its date. No prediction is quietly dropped; UNTESTED must say
   why it went unmeasured.
2. Revise: a broken prediction changes the hypothesis text in a numbered
   version, stating what changed and why. A confirmed one gets folded into
   the evidence lines.
3. Register the next block's predictions BEFORE its day 1, in this file.
4. The flexibility battery contributes its pattern reading each time it is
   run — it is the highest-resolution instrument this system has for the
   guarding-vs-length question (P6).

**What Version 1 already commits to for Block B, if the block-A trials
confirm:** preparation keeps shifting from release toward activation; passive
stretching narrows to the measured-short list (adductors) while everything
else trains at end range under load; stillness management (desk exposure,
movement breaks) is treated as the primary symptom lever, not training
modification; and loading progresses on measured capacity rather than
symptom-era caution.
