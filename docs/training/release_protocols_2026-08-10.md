# Two release protocols, run as hypothesis tests

**Authored:** 2026-08-10, on the athlete's direction, from the physiotherapist's
2026-08-10 answers (`patient_profile.py` `symptom_log` 2026-08-10), built with
`Input_files/cluster_d_mechanics.md` / `cluster_d_prescription.md` (the shoulder
cluster source) and `Input_files/baar_tendon_annex.md` (conduct rules, §4).
**Status:** physio-cleared for self-directed use (the pec protocol explicitly;
the hip protocol recommended by the physio in the same response). Neither is a
training stressor — both sit in the release/pre-session family, ≤10 minutes,
~50% effort, and neither touches the one-new-stressor-per-week budget.

Both are HYPOTHESIS TESTS in the `HRV_GARMIN_HOLD` idiom: the expected outcome,
the instrument, the timescale and the stop conditions are written down BEFORE
the first session, so a borderline result cannot be read toward the answer
already in mind.

---

## Protocol 1 — Right pec/scar release + anterior-wall active reciprocation

**What the physio prescribed:** targeted scar & pectoralis minor self-myofascial
release, plus subscapularis / anterior-wall active reciprocation with isometric
holds. Diagnosis: positional ischemic cramp / scar adhesion lockout — shortened
tissue firing hard in a shortened range, catching on dense surgical scar tissue.

**The hypothesis (H1).** The lock-out is maintained by scar adhesion plus high
resting tone in a shortened range. Daily release applied through movement, plus
active work biased toward the LONG position (the opposite of the position that
cramps), reduces the cramp's intensity or delays its onset **within two weeks**
— the Cluster D §F timescale: fibrosis responds in weeks, sometimes sessions,
or it is not the limiter.

**The instrument.** The prayer position itself, standardised: hands together at
the chest, fingers forward, forearms ~45° up — the same angle every time. Ease
into it, note **seconds until the lock-out starts** and **intensity 0–10**.
Capture the baseline ONCE before the first release session. Then re-check
**weekly, on the same weekday** — never daily; a provocation test repeated
daily becomes its own irritant and its own noise.

### Technique A — scar & pec minor release (right side, daily, ~3 min)

- **Position:** stand facing a wall. A massage ball sits between the wall and
  the upper-right chest, just below the outer third of the collarbone, angled
  in toward the bony knob at the front of the shoulder. For the scar itself:
  lie on your back and use fingertips instead of the ball.
- **Movement:** this is not rolling. Pin one tender spot with steady pressure,
  then MOVE THE ARM slowly — from resting at your side to reaching forward and
  slightly up, palm turning out — 5–6 slow passes, then move to the next spot.
  On the scar: fingertip pressure beside the scar line (not raking along it),
  small slow circles and gentle skin-glide in each direction, working down each
  side of the scar.
- **Feel:** deep pressure that "talks back" — the cramping, good-hurt quality of
  a knot under pressure, breathable throughout, easing within 30–60 seconds of
  sustained pressure. Pain never above 2/10.
- **Stop:** any point-specific ice-pick sensation; any tingling, numbness or
  ache running into the arm (that is nerve — move off the spot immediately);
  any pulse under the pressure (move off — that is a vessel, not muscle). Never
  press into the hollow of the armpit.
- **Progress:** spots that were tender going quiet is the local endpoint. When
  a week of sessions finds nothing that talks back, drop to twice a week.

### Technique B — anterior-wall active reciprocation (right side, ~5 min, after A)

Built from Cluster D §E's stabilised-shoulder position and the annex §4 conduct
rules. The arm stays LOW throughout — never external rotation at 90° abduction,
which is the apprehension position the Latarjet exists to protect.

- **Position:** seated, right elbow resting on the right knee, forearm hanging,
  elbow and shoulder in one vertical line — the arm low and close to the body.
- **Movement:** one cycle = (1) press the palm gently inward against the other
  hand — the direction the tight anterior wall pulls — ramping in over 3–5
  seconds, holding 5 seconds at "tension, not max" (~50%, no shaking), ramping
  out over 3–5 seconds; (2) then actively rotate the forearm OUTWARD to its
  comfortable end range under its own power and HOLD there — 10 seconds at
  first, building toward 30 over the two weeks (long-standing tissue takes the
  long end of the hold range). 3 cycles. Across the 3 cycles, vary the elbow:
  bent 90°, more open, more closed — biased toward the positions where the
  front of the shoulder feels LONG, which is the opposite of the position that
  cramps.
- **Feel:** work, then a diffuse warm stretch-effort at the front of the
  shoulder in the outward hold. A warm burn is acceptable; the lock-out cramp
  appearing means the position has drifted too short — open the angle.
- **Stop:** ice-pick; anything into the arm; any hard, abrupt, unspringy
  end-feel on the outward rotation (that is capsule or bone on a stabilised
  shoulder — do not push on it, it may be doing load-bearing work); any
  instability sensation.
- **Progress:** longer outward holds (10 → 30 s), then — only at the block
  build — a light loaded hold per Cluster D §E.

### Verdict table (pre-registered)

| Outcome at 2 weeks (re-check ~2026-08-24) | Reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Onset later or intensity down ≥2 points | H1 supported — adhesion/tone was the maintainer | Continue; taper release as spots go quiet; fold Technique B into the block |
| Unchanged | §F's own answer: self-release insufficient | Take to the physio for professional soft-tissue treatment — the reference results for hands-on work were fast and well-retained |
| Any pain, neural sign, or instability | Wrong tool or wrong tissue | STOP both techniques, physio, record in symptom_log |

## Protocol 2 — Anterior-hip sustained pressure (the sitting-pressure hypothesis)

**What the physio recommended:** "sustained pressure testing for the front of
the hip as well, to release the pressure from sitting."

**The hypothesis (H2).** The desk day holds the hip-flexor complex short for
6–8 hours — wear, not training. Part of the seated anterior-tilt restriction
(the battery's central deficit) is therefore held TONE, not just tissue length.
If that is true, sustained-pressure release produces two observable effects:
(a) locally, tender points at the front of the hip that quiet over ~2 weeks,
with easier standing-up-straight after sitting; (b) globally, it acts as an
adjunct multiplier on the F-stack's tilt work. If the front of the hip has no
tender points that respond, H2 is wrong for this body and the protocol retires.

**Sequencing — the battery comes first, and this is not negotiable.** The
seated tilt is the battery's measured deficit, and the three baseline mornings
define the noise floor every later comparison depends on. Starting a new
intervention before the baseline is captured contaminates the one measurement
this project has been protecting for weeks. Order: (1) three cold baseline
mornings; (2) battery run, on a morning not preceded by leg training; (3) THIS
PROTOCOL STARTS THE DAY AFTER the battery is captured. The pec protocol has no
such interaction and starts immediately.

### Technique — front-of-hip sustained pressure (~4 min, daily on desk days)

- **Position:** lying face down, a massage ball between the floor and the
  front of the hip. Two target zones, worked one at a time: (1) the meaty
  pocket-corner region just below the pelvic crest at the FRONT-OUTSIDE of the
  hip; (2) slightly inward and higher, just inside the point of the hip bone.
- **Movement:** settle body weight onto the ball slowly and wait — 60–90
  seconds of stillness per spot, breathing, until the tissue visibly lets go
  under you. Then a few slow knee-bends of that leg with the pressure held,
  and move to the next spot.
- **Feel:** the cramping good-hurt of a held knot, easing as you breathe.
  Pain never above 2/10.
- **Stop — and this is the sharp edge of this protocol:** the inner-front of
  the hip carries the leg's main artery and nerve. NEVER press where you feel
  a pulse. NEVER continue into tingling, numbness or electric sensation down
  the leg — move off immediately. Stay on the OUTER half of the front of the
  hip, on tissue that talks back like muscle; the crease at the very front of
  the hip, middle third, is off-limits entirely.
- **Progress:** tender points quieting; standing up straight after sitting
  getting unremarkable.

### Verdict table (pre-registered)

| Outcome | Reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Tender points found, quiet over ~2 weeks, sitting-to-standing eases | H2 supported locally | Continue as a desk-day standing item; credit tilt-angle gains to the F-STACK primarily — this is an adjunct, and the attribution is pre-declared |
| No tender points, or no change in anything by ~4 weeks | H2 wrong for this body | Retire the protocol, record it in the profile — a null is informative |
| Any neural sign, any pulse-adjacent pressure event | Technique fault | Stop, re-read the stop rules, resume only clear of the inner zone |

**What this protocol does NOT claim:** that it lengthens anything, or that a
tilt-angle improvement at the four-week retest belongs to it. The F-stack
trains the tilt; this releases what sitting holds. The battery adjudicates the
tilt; this protocol's own instruments are the tender points and the
after-sitting feel.

## Shared conduct rules (annex §4, restated once)

Pain ≤2/10 always. Ice-pick = stop; diffuse warm burn = acceptable. Ramp every
isometric in and out over 3–5 seconds — never enter a hold abruptly. ~50%
effort, no shaking, never max. Everything here fits inside a ~10-minute dose,
which is all the connective-tissue signal accepts before it saturates anyway.
