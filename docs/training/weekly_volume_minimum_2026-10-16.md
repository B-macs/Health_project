# The minimum weekly dose — evidence note, 2026-10-16

*The athlete, 2026-10-16: "Then our goal is too high for sets per week, Research
and find out what is the minimum. I'd like to aim for a 3 day week meets the
required goal and a 4th day is a bonus" — and "We might be taking on too many
areas at once as well, and we need to drop one area for now ... This is also an
option."* Step 3 of `docs/training/goal_system.md` had set 10 hard sets a week
for the priority-1 areas and 6 for the rest; placed on four sessions, that came
to 67-70 minutes each, and a missed session left five or six areas short.

## What the research says (checked 2026-10-16)

| Source | What it is | What it found |
|---|---|---|
| Pelland, Remmert, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos, *Sports Med* 2025 ([DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w](https://colab.ws/articles/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w); [preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460)) | Meta-regression, 67 studies, 2,058 people | More weekly sets give more growth and more strength, with diminishing returns — strength flattens much sooner than size. Counting an indirect set as half a set ("fractional", the counting step 3 already uses) predicted the results best. With volume equal, more days a week made no clear difference to size; strength rose with frequency, with diminishing returns. The abstract gives no numeric threshold; secondary reports of one were not checked. |
| Remmert, Pelland et al. ([preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)) | Meta-regression, sets per session | No added benefit could be detected past about 2 direct sets per session for strength and about 11 fractional sets per session for size. |
| Iversen, Norum, Schoenfeld & Fimland, *Sports Med* 2021;51:2079-2095 ([PMC8449772](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772)) | Narrative review, time-efficient training | **At least 4 hard sets per muscle group a week**, 6-15 rep loads near failure, is the practical minimum for growth; weekly volume matters more than frequency; supersets roughly halve the time. A recommendation, not a measured threshold. |
| Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger, *J Sports Sci* 2017;35(11) ([PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Dose-response%20relationship%20between%20weekly%20resistance%20training%20volume%20and%20increases%20in%20muscle%20mass%20-%20A%20systematic%20review%20and%20metaanalysis.pdf)) | Meta-analysis, 15 studies, mostly untrained | Growth of 5.4% under 5 sets a week, 6.6% at 5-9, 9.8% at 10 or more (p = 0.074). The low band still grew muscle. |
| Androulakis-Korakakis, Fisher & Steele, *Sports Med* 2020 ([summary](https://www.ufrgs.br/sees-initiative/pmid31797219/)) | Systematic review, trained men | **One hard set per exercise, 2-3 times a week, raises the squat and bench 1RM** — "suboptimal, yet significant". |
| Spiering et al., *J Strength Cond Res* 2021 ([PubMed 33629972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33629972/)) | Narrative review, maintenance | Strength and size kept for up to 32 weeks with one session a week and one set per exercise, if the load stays high (younger people; older people need about two sessions and 2-3 sets). |
| ACSM position stand 2026 (Phillips et al., *Med Sci Sports Exerc*; [ACSM summary](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years)) | Overview of 137 systematic reviews | About **10 sets per muscle a week to make the most of growth**; train every major muscle at least twice a week; any regular training helps. Read in secondary coverage only. |

**Read together:** 10 sets a week is the figure for the MOST growth, not the
least that works. About 4 hard sets a week is the practical floor for growth,
and strength needs even less, so long as the sets are hard and the load stays
up. Between 4 and 10 the returns shrink with every set.

## The proposal (for his approval)

- **Required — met by three sessions:** priority-1 areas (glutes, abs, deep
  back, hamstrings) **6 hard sets a week on 2 days** (above the floor of 4, in
  the 5-9 band); priority-2 areas (quads, chest, back, press, shoulder blades,
  side and rear delts) **4 a week**, counting half-sets; the hip flexors'
  strength-at-length work 3; the hip airplane his 3 × max on 2 days; the
  adductor ladder at home as before.
- **The fourth session is a bonus:** it adds one more appearance of each
  exercise in the drop order, highest priority first, until it reaches 60
  minutes, and never repeats a heavy lift from the day before.

Measured with the placement rules on the draft cards (2026-10-16): the three
core sessions take 54, 57 and 59 minutes and meet every required number; the
bonus session takes 60 and lifts abs to 12, deep back to 9, glutes to 16.5,
hamstrings to 9, quads to 9 and hip flexors to 6.

**Dropping an area is not needed to fit the time** under this dose. If he wants
fewer areas for focus, the drop order's lowest area is the side delts; without
them the core sessions take 51, 51 and 59 minutes.
