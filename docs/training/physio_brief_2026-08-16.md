## 9. Specialization cycle — asking for the boundary, not for sign-off now

**Not an ask for this block.** Raised so the boundary is on the record before
it is wanted, rather than argued about later.

The prompt is the daily-frequency experiment popularised in mid-2026 (n=3,
one side trained daily for 30 days vs. twice weekly, MRI'd at both ends).
Its finding: muscle tolerated the daily dose fine — **connective tissue did
not**. It cites a 2024 study in which trained lifters benched daily for 34
days, all gained strength, and **over half were injured**. The recommendation
that came out of it was a *specialization cycle* — 2–3 hard sets of one
lagging muscle at the start of every training day, rotating two exercises to
spare the tendons.

**Why it is being flagged rather than adopted.** The one tissue that failed in
that experiment is the one this profile is pre-selected for: hypermobility
(Beighton 6/9) means stability is muscular rather than ligamentous, the right
shoulder already had a soft-tissue repair fail before the Latarjet, and the
lumbar spine carries three protrusions with activated L5/S1 osteochondrosis.
Separately, the mechanism does not match the gap in section 4 — that
experiment's lever is weekly hard-set volume for *hypertrophy*, whereas the
interscapular finding here is **endurance**, and the trunk finding in the 2025
log is **recruitment timing under fatigue** ("glutes don't finish the
lockout", "deep core turns off under fatigue"). More hard sets is the wrong
lever for both.

**Athlete's decision, 2026-08-05:** revisit after the 10 km on **2026-10-11**,
not at this reassessment — the running introduction and the Stage 2B decision
are already landing here, and a fourth simultaneous change makes attribution
impossible when something flares. First trial to be **arms only, 1–3 months**,
chosen because it is the lowest-consequence target available (no spinal
loading, no scapular loading, currently **0 direct sets/week**) — so a failed
trial costs nothing. Expand to other regions only if that trial is clean.

**The question for you:** given the connective-tissue picture, is a
higher-frequency specialization block ever appropriate here, and if so what
is the boundary — which regions, what frequency ceiling, and what would you
want to see before it touches anything spinal or scapular?

For reference, current loaded weekly volume in this block is chest 3 hard
sets, arms 0 direct, quads 6, glutes/hamstrings 9, back 9, plus scapular work

## 10. Tendon loading — bearing directly on the §1 ask

Added 2026-08-05 from a second source (a popular summary of Keith Baar's
tendon-loading work, UC Davis). It is included because it bears on the §1
isometric-holds ask specifically, and because the athlete has asked for a view
rather than adopting anything from it.

**What is worth taking from it.** The best-supported claim is that tendon
becomes **refractory to further loading stimulus for several hours** after
roughly ten minutes of loading — which argues for short, frequent doses rather
than one long session. If §1 is signed off, this is a dosing consideration you
may want to fold into the prescription.

**What is not worth taking from it.** The "creep progressively recruits
healthy then damaged fibres" mechanism is a plausible story, not established.
And its "pain resolves in one to two weeks" claim traces to the 2015 patellar
tendinopathy isometric-analgesia work, which has **not replicated reliably** —
later trials found isometrics no better than isotonic loading for pain. It is
presented here as one loading mode among several, not a special pain switch.
Its own strongest caveat is that isometrics are the early-rehab tool, while
long-term tendon capacity comes from heavy slow resistance and loaded
lengthened positions — i.e. largely the block already running.

**Where it does and does not map onto this profile.**

| Finding | Fit |
|---|---|
| Coxa saltans, right hip (#4) | A real tendon phenomenon — iliopsoas over the iliopectineal eminence — but **painless on every attempt**. The source's own rule is that clicking without pain is benign. Not proposed for loading. |
| Interscapular (§2–§4 above) | The consolidated finding is muscular **endurance**, not tendinopathy. Isometrics may still be the right tool, but for a different reason than this source gives. |
| Proximal hamstring at the ischial tuberosity (#2) | The one genuine tendon-insertion candidate in the profile. Mid-range isometrics are conventional first line. **Worth your view** — and relatedly, whether the existing "Ischial Tuberosity Hamstring Release" (sustained pressure, currently in the pre-session protocol on Days 3 and 10) is the right handling for that site. |
| Lumbar spine | Disc, endplate and bone. Nothing in this source reaches it. |

**The tension we would like settled rather than assumed.** This profile's
documented worst load case is **sustained low-load holding** — the 2026-07-06
QL strain came from holding a posture for the length of a walk, and the
interscapular pattern in §2 is driven by roughly eight hours of continuous
holding. Adding isometric holding into that is not obviously wrong (30 s at
high intent is a different exposure from 8 h at trivial load) but it is not
obviously safe either, and a twice-daily dose lands on top of a desk day that
is already the identified driver. If §1 is approved, we would rather have the
dose, the intent level and the daily frequency come from you than infer them.

**Also relevant, no sign-off needed:** the same source names load management —
keeping weekly increases under ~30% — as the single biggest tendon mistake.
That is already implemented in this app as the ACWR ceiling of 1.3 for the
current stage, but enforcement is presently held in advisory mode (it reports
a breach without capping volume). Restoring hard enforcement is a
software-side decision the athlete controls, and it needs no prescription
change.
