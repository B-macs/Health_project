## Against the athlete's rules (checked 2026-10-16)

- **Finding #4, the right hip click:** bending the right hip and turning it out
  sets off the click (Key Rule 7: neutral or inward rotation above 60° of hip
  bend). The seated cable hip rotation turns the hip out with the hip bent to
  90°, so its outward turn on the right is out. The prone version keeps the hips
  straight and passes.
- **Already in the week:** the 90/90 hip rotation with lift-offs runs in the
  Sunday flexibility session.
- **Bands** give no tension at the stretch and sit below the A tier.
- **Gym:** the cable stack and benches are confirmed; the prone rotation needs
  an ankle cuff.
- **Safety rules:** the prone cable hip rotation reads "unknown" and gets a rule
  before it is used.

**Check of the pick (step 8, 2026-10-16): the prone cable hip rotation (S)
ranks above the hip airplane (A)** — load in small steps on the stack (the
airplane can only add reps) and tension where the rotators are longest. What
it gives up: the airplane is the only exercise here that turns the pelvis on a
standing leg, the movement of the twist. Turning both ways with both legs costs
about 5 more minutes on each of its two days. **KEPT BY HIS CHOICE, 2026-10-16:**
*"No - we can add weight to this by holding it around the back but I like the
balance part of this as well as the rotation."* The hip airplane stays, with a
weight held behind the back in both hands (so no hand on the wall: the balance
is part of the exercise) and a rep range, so the one progression rule adds
weight to it like any other lift.
