# Hamstrings — tier list

- **Source (ranked):** a YouTube "hams and glutes" tier-list video, pasted by the
  athlete on 2026-10-15 (video 3 of 6; no URL). The transcript names no channel.
  It is probably the channel of video 1 (the "hamstrings and glutes simplified"
  video below): the same closing lines and the same RDL explanation. Reasons are
  short paraphrases.
- **Scale:** S → A → B → C → D. One tier word is unreadable in the transcript
  (#12).
- **The source's criteria:** none stated, and no study is cited. The reasons are
  the presenter's view of stretch, feel, stability, how easy the exercise is to
  load, and risk.
- **One list for two areas:** the source ranks hamstring and glute exercises
  together, "because they always work together", plus two lower-back
  exercises. The FULL list is here. Its glute rows are also in
  [glutes.md](glutes.md) and its lower-back rows in
  [deep_back.md](deep_back.md), so that step 5 reads each row in its own
  area's file.
- **Best:** hinge — the Romanian deadlift; isolation — the lying leg curl. The
  source calls the Romanian deadlift and the hip thrust the best of all its
  exercises.

## Against the athlete's rules (checked 2026-10-15)

- **Barbells are out** (his rule, 2026-10-15): #4 and #9 as barbell lifts. The
  Romanian deadlift and the stiff-leg deadlift stay in with dumbbells.
- **Safety rules:** the Romanian deadlift and the single-leg Romanian deadlift are
  "caution" (allowed). The good morning is "contraindicated". The
  hyperextension family is "caution" and goes only to a straight line (the
  lumbar-extension rule). The stiff-leg deadlift, both leg curls, the Nordic curl
  and the glute-ham raise are "unknown" — each needs a rule before it is picked.
- **Gym:** seated and lying leg curl machines confirmed (2026-10-15).
- **Conflict with a trial — seated against lying curl:** this list puts the
  lying curl (S) above the seated curl (A), on feel. A 12-week trial found more
  hamstring growth with the seated curl (Maeo et al. 2021, Med Sci Sports Exerc;
  checked 2026-10-15). Which one wins is the open rule for two sources on one
  question (`docs/training/goal_system.md`, step 5).

**Picks now:** the dumbbell Romanian deadlift (S). The leg curl: seated or
lying — OPEN until that rule is decided.

## Unranked source advice

- **Video 1 of 2026-10-15 — "hamstrings and glutes simplified"** (probably the
  same channel as the list above):
  - One hip hinge plus one curl. Hinges: Romanian deadlift, stiff-leg deadlift,
    good morning. Curl: the machine; without one, the Nordic curl. A curl alone
    is not enough.
  - Two sessions a week; about 12 sets a week (6 hinge, 6 curl), with no reason
    given to go above 12.
  - Reps: the hinge 5-15; the curl 8-30, mostly 12-20.
  - The point of a hinge is a full stretch in the hamstrings.
  - Romanian against stiff-leg deadlift: the Romanian starts at the top with the
    weight close to the shins and the knees a little more bent; the stiff-leg
    starts from the floor, the weight further away, the legs almost straight.
- **Jeff Nippard, leg day (recorded 2026-10-15):**
  - **Seated leg curl** — cites the 2021 trial above: more growth than the
    lying curl, because the seated position trains the hamstrings longer
    (checked: 20 people, 12 weeks, more growth in the hamstring heads that
    cross the hip). Set the seat so the hamstrings feel stretched at the start.
  - **Romanian deadlift** — for the hamstrings, cut the top 10-25% of the lift
    and pause at the bottom; keep the weight close to the legs.
  - **Glute-ham raise**, with the top quarter left out (no hamstring tension
    there); the **stiff-leg deadlift** and the **Nordic curl** as alternatives.
- **Built With Science, leg workout (recorded 2026-10-15; pasted again as video
  4 of 6 — the same video):**
  - **Glute-ham raise** — cites an EMG study with the highest hamstring activity
    of the common hamstring exercises, the leg curl and the Romanian deadlift
    included. It trains the lowering phase on every rep, which the video links
    to performance and to fewer hamstring injuries. Knees just behind the pad;
    head, back and hips in one line; lower under control; 10-15 reps. Without
    the machine: the same movement kneeling on a bosu ball with the feet
    against a wall, lowering only at first.
  - The second leg day of the week should hold a hip-hinge hamstring exercise.
