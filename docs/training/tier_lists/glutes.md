# Glutes — tier list

- **Source:** a YouTube glute tier-list video (Jeff Nippard — the transcript
  names his nutrition app's code), pasted by the athlete on 2026-10-15 (video 3
  of 5; no URL given). Reasons are short paraphrases.
- **Scale:** S (best) → A → B → C → D → F. The source adds "high" and "low"
  inside a tier; they are kept. One exercise is raised to **S+**.
- **The source's three criteria:**
  1. High tension, above all in the stretched position.
  2. It feels good: no knee, hip or lower-back pain.
  3. A way to progress it over time.
- **Regions:** the source splits the glutes into upper (glute medius and
  minimus), middle and lower, and names a best exercise for each.
- **Best overall (S+):** walking lunge. **Worst:** donkey kick or fire hydrant.
- **Best by region:** upper — machine hip abduction; middle — machine hip
  thrust; lower — Romanian deadlift.

## The full list, in the source's order

| # | Exercise | Tier | Region emphasis | Source's main reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Barbell hip thrust | high B | Whole, mainly middle | Well studied, easy to progress; the bar can pinch, slow set-up, little stretch |
| 2 | Machine hip thrust (belt type) | high A | Middle — **best for the middle glutes** | Comfortable, no set-up, stable; lacks a deep stretch |
| 3 | Single-leg dumbbell hip thrust | A | Whole, some upper | Balance brings in the upper glutes; trains each side alone |
| 4 | Glute bridge | low B | — | A hip thrust with less range |
| 5 | Frog pumps | high C | Upper | Hard to load, little stretch; a high-rep finisher |
| 6 | Barbell squat | A | Whole, mainly middle | Big stretch, strong overload; also loads the quads heavily |
| 7 | Smith machine squat, feet forward | A | Whole | Feet forward makes it more glute-dominant; no balance demand |
| 8 | Bulgarian split squat, front foot forward, leaning forward | high A | Middle and upper | Deeper glute stretch than a squat; hard to load, very tiring |
| 9 | Donkey kick | low D | — | Little tension in the stretch, hard to load; a warm-up drill |
| 10 | Fire hydrant | low D | — | Same problems; for mobility or rehab |
| 11 | Kickback, cable or machine, braced | low A | Upper, if kicked up and out | Stable, smooth; a small stretch |
| 12 | Step-up, box at or above knee height | low A | Upper and whole | Deep stretch; balance brings in the upper glutes |
| 13 | Machine hip abduction, leaning forward about 30° | **S** | Upper — **best for the upper glutes** | Trains glute medius and minimus directly; easy to load |
| 14 | Cable hip abduction | B | Upper | Same job, harder to load, less stable |
| 15 | Lateral band walk | C | Upper | Almost no tension in the stretch; a warm-up drill |
| 16 | Walking lunge, long strides, slight forward lean | **S+** | Whole — **best overall** | Deep stretch; the presenter's most effective glute exercise |
| 17 | Smith machine lunge | high A | Whole | Same benefits, done on the spot |
| 18 | Smith machine lunge, front foot on a small box | **S** | Whole | The extra range at the bottom lifts it to S |
| 19 | Curtsy lunge | low B | Upper | More glute medius, harder to load |
| 20 | Conventional deadlift | low B | Lower | Big stretch, great overload, but very fatiguing |
| 21 | Sumo deadlift | high B | Lower and upper | Similar glute max activity; more glute medius from the wide stance |
| 22 | Romanian deadlift | high A | Lower — **best for the lower glutes** | Hits the glute-hamstring tie-in; easy to overload |
| 23 | 45° back extension, upper back rounded | **S** | Whole | The glutes work in both the long and the short position |
| 24 | Cable pull-through | low B | — | Good for beginners; awkward when heavy |
| 25 | Kettlebell swing | low D | — | Cardio and power; too little tension for growth |

## Against the athlete's rules (checked 2026-10-15)

- **Barbells are out:** #1, #6, #20, #21 as barbell lifts.
- **No direct work for the upper glutes** (his step-2 decision, 2026-10-14: they
  are on his list of tense muscles and settle when the weak muscles take their
  share): rules out #13 (S), #14, #5 and #15.
- **The 45° back extension** (#23) goes only to a straight line, never past it
  (the back-extension safety rule at L5/S1).
- **Safety-rule gap:** "walking lunge" matches the rule for WALKING and comes
  back "cleared" — a false clearance. A lunge rule is needed before it is used.

## Unranked source advice

From leg-workout videos that rank nothing (pasted 2026-10-15):

- The hip thrust as the complement to the squat; one 12-week study cited there
  found squats grew the glutes about twice as much as hip thrusts (Built With
  Science, leg workout).
- Split squat, progressing to the Bulgarian split squat, then a dumbbell in the
  opposite hand to bring in the glute medius (Built With Science).
- The dumbbell walking lunge at full depth, lowering under control, with straps
  if the grip fails first (Jeff Nippard, leg day 1).
