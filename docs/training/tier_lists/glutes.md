# Glutes — tier list

- **Source:** a YouTube glute tier-list video (Jeff Nippard — the transcript
  names his nutrition app's code), pasted by the athlete on 2026-10-15 (video 3
  of 5; no URL given). Reasons are short paraphrases. The same video was pasted
  again later that day (video 2 of 6): checked row by row, no change.
- **Scale:** S (best) → A → B → C → D → F. The source adds "high" and "low"
  inside a tier; they are kept. One exercise is raised to **S+**.
- **The source's three criteria:**
  1. High tension, above all in the stretched position.
  2. It feels good: no knee, hip or lower-back pain.
  3. A way to progress it over time.
- **Regions:** the source splits the glutes into upper (glute medius and
  minimus), middle and lower, and names a best exercise for each.
- **Best overall (S+):** walking lunge. **Worst:** donkey kick or fire hydrant.
- **Best by region:** upper — machine hip abduction; middle — machine hip
  thrust; lower — Romanian deadlift.
- **A second ranked list** covers the glutes too — the hams-and-glutes list,
  whose full text is in [hamstrings.md](hamstrings.md). Its glute rows are below
  the first list, with where the two lists disagree.

## Against the athlete's rules (checked 2026-10-15)

- **Barbells are out:** #1, #6, #20, #21 as barbell lifts.
- **No direct work for the upper glutes** (his step-2 decision, 2026-10-14: they
  are on his list of tense muscles and settle when the weak muscles take their
  share): rules out #13 (S), #14, #5 and #15.
- **The 45° back extension** (#23) goes only to a straight line, never past it
  (the back-extension safety rule at L5/S1).
- **Safety-rule gap:** "walking lunge" matches the rule for WALKING and comes
  back "cleared" — a false clearance. A lunge rule is needed before it is used.
- **Safety rules, second list (checked 2026-10-15):** the hip thrust (machine
  and single-leg), the reverse lunge, the step-up and the cable kickback are
  "unknown" — each needs a rule before it is picked. The Romanian deadlift is
  "caution" (allowed); the good morning is "contraindicated".
- **Upper-glute rows of the second list:** none — its kickback is out under the
  same step-2 decision, and it is B there anyway.

## Unranked source advice

- **Built With Science, leg workout** (recorded 2026-10-15; pasted again as
  video 4 of 6 — the same video):
  - The hip thrust as the complement to the squat: it loads the glutes most at
    the top, where the squat does not, and is easy on the joints and the lower
    back. Tips: tilt the pelvis back at the top, do not arch the lower back,
    shins vertical at the top; 12-15 reps.
  - It cites a 12-week trial in trained women where squats grew the glutes about
    twice as much as hip thrusts (Barbalho et al. 2020, Int J Sports Med).
    **Checked 2026-10-15:** the paper is not retracted, but journals retracted
    other papers by its first author over their data, and this one carries the
    same doubt. A 9-week trial in untrained people found the two about equal
    (cited in Jeff Nippard's list above). Read as: no clear winner.
  - Split squat first: in a study it cites, beginners on walking lunges lost
    balance, cut the range short and used less glute medius than on split
    squats. Then the Bulgarian split squat, then a dumbbell in the opposite hand
    to bring in the glute medius; 8-12 reps.
- **Jeff Nippard, leg day 1:** the dumbbell walking lunge at full depth, lowering
  under control, with straps if the grip fails first.
- **Video 1 of 2026-10-15 — "hamstrings and glutes simplified":**
  - One compound exercise (the hip thrust, done first) and one isolation
    exercise (butt blaster, kickbacks, or lunges with long steps); deep squats
    add glute work.
  - One or two glute sessions a week; 6-12 sets a week on top of the leg-day
    work.
  - Hip thrust 8-12 reps: all the way up, hold the top about a second, no
    bounce. Isolation 12-30 reps.
- **Built With Science — glute EMG test of 17 exercises** (video 5 of 6): 4
  people, men and a woman, loads matched for effort, one set of each. The
  presenter says himself that more activity does not always mean more growth.
  - The hip thrust gave the highest activity in the lower and the upper glutes,
    but it loads them most at the top, in the short position; the video takes
    the stretched position to matter more for growth.
  - Lower glutes: squats, step-ups, lunges and Bulgarian split squats with a
    slight forward lean and a vertical shin; and deadlifts, conventional and
    Romanian.
  - The Bulgarian split squat and the reverse lunge with the front foot on a
    step stretch the glutes most at the bottom; beginners start with the plain
    reverse lunge or split squat.
  - Upper glutes: the kickback angled about 30° out, hip abduction, and a
    reverse lunge stepping back and to the side — all direct upper-glute work,
    OUT under his step-2 decision.
  - Order: a hip-extension exercise first (done fresh, the most important for
    growth), then the hip thrust, then upper-glute work (out for him).
  - Men and women gave similar results: the same exercises, different
    programming.
