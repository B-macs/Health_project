# Shoulders (deltoids) — tier list

- **Source (ranked):** Jeff Nippard, YouTube shoulder tier list (the transcript
  names his book), pasted by the athlete on 2026-10-15 (no URL). Reasons are
  short paraphrases.
- **Scale:** S → A → B → C → D. A "+" marks the top of a tier; S+ is the best
  overall.
- **The source's three criteria:** high tension, above all in the stretched
  position; it feels good (no shoulder pain, a smooth resistance curve); a
  simple progression.
- **Heads:** front, side and rear. Best per head: front — machine shoulder
  press; side — cable lateral raise; rear — reverse cable crossover. **Best
  overall (S+):** cable lateral raise. **Worst:** front raise — not useless,
  but redundant when you press.
- The presenter favours side-delt work over front-delt work, because pressing
  already trains the front delts.
- **His areas (2026-10-15):** the front delts are part of the press muscles
  (goal B); the side and rear delts became areas of their own (*"Yes side and
  rear delts are nearly more important for me"*). Dose: 6 hard sets a week
  each (his decision 2026-10-16, `docs/training/goal_system.md`, step 3).

## Unranked source advice

From two shoulder videos that rank nothing (pasted 2026-10-15):

- **Built With Science — two shoulder exercises:**
  - The cable lateral raise in the line the arm naturally rises in (about 30°
    in front of the body), the cable set a little above the floor, and the arm
    allowed behind the body for more stretch; one or both arms. Without
    cables: dumbbell lateral raises followed by partial reps at the bottom with
    heavier dumbbells, or the lean-in dumbbell lateral raise.
  - For the rear part of the side delt: a cable raise with the body turned
    about 45° toward the cable, or a chest-supported dumbbell raise on an
    incline bench one notch below vertical.
  - Pressing already gives the front delts far more weekly work than the side
    delts.
  - Progression: 10-20 reps; add weight once 15 reps are reached on every set;
    small plates on the stack pin for smaller jumps.
  - Cites an 8-week study (25 trained people, one arm on each exercise): cables
    and dumbbells grew the side delts about the same at one measuring site,
    with some advantage for cables at the other.
- **Jeff Nippard — seven shoulder tips:**
  - Keep the upper traps out of lateral raises: sweep the weight out to the
    side with the shoulders down.
  - Train the delts 3-4 times a week: about 6-8 effective sets per muscle per
    session, beyond which more adds little.
  - One heavy shoulder press (machine, dumbbell or barbell) at 5-6 reps, a
    small weekly increase, a lighter week every 4-8 weeks. In his own EMG test
    the machine press worked the side delts strongly.
  - Train at long muscle lengths too: press deeper; cross-body cable lateral
    raises; cross-body Y raises; rear-delt work with the arms crossing over.
  - Double progression for isolation work (e.g. 10-12 reps, add a rep, then
    weight); change the exercise when it stalls.
  - He describes the same 8-week cable-versus-dumbbell study as showing no
    difference. **The two videos read that study differently; not checked.**

## Against the athlete's rules (checked 2026-10-15)

- **The right shoulder's limits — one measured, one written:**
  - **Measured:** limited range with the arm behind the body — a face-down arm
    raise reached 20 cm on the right against 40 cm on the left (2026-08-13).
  - **Written, never measured:** "never load external rotation at 90° of
    abduction" (the arm out to the side at shoulder height and turned out). It
    comes from the Cluster D shoulder documents (`Input_files/`, added
    2026-08-06): a general precaution for a shoulder that dislocates forward,
    to hold "until a physio clears" the position. It was copied into the
    safety rules the same day and into the release protocol on 2026-08-10. No
    surgeon, physiotherapist or test in his record set it; the physiotherapist
    was never asked, and the physio gate was retired on 2026-08-17. His
    question of 2026-10-15 (*"Where did that safety limit come from ?"*).
    **REMOVED 2026-10-16, his decision** (*"remove it, I don't have any
    problems with this and have been doing shoulder presses for years without
    issue"*) — details in `goal_system.md`, goal B.
  - **Stays out, his precaution (2026-10-16, confirmed):** pulldowns and
    presses behind the head, dips, and the deep wide-grip bench.
- **Effects of the MEASURED limit (the arm behind the body):**
  - #15 behind-the-back lateral raise starts with the arm behind the body —
    ranks down on the right.
  - #18 and #21 rear-delt flyes: the right arm stops at the line of the body.
- **Effects of the WRITTEN limit — all LIFTED 2026-10-16:**
  - #14 cable Y raise — back in on the right.
  - #19 face pull — keeps the video's turned-out finish; the right elbow stops
    at the line of the body (the measured limit).
  - #13 cable lateral raise — done as the source describes it; no thumb rule.
  - The bottom of a shoulder press (#7, #8 and the goal-B proof) — no
    conflict any more. The 2025 log holds his seated dumbbell press up to
    15 kg × 9 and no dislocation; none since the Latarjet (about 2016).
- **Agreed 2026-10-15 (*"Yes agree"*):** the limits on the picks — the cable
  lateral raise to shoulder height, thumb not turned up; the reverse cable
  crossover, the right arm stopping at the line of the body; the face pull
  without the turned-out finish. He agreed before he knew where the written
  limit came from. **Changed 2026-10-16** when he removed it: the first and
  the last are lifted, and the crossover's limit stays (it is the measured

  once again from 2026-10-29** (his note: *"Reverse cable cross over is now done
